The Conformal Rosetta

Connecting the threads: how conformal maps bridge the mathematical ideas in the 37∞ Lab.

Euler Spiral ↔ Homotopy

∫₀ˢ eit² dt

The Euler spiral is a homotopy between a straight line (s=0, zero curvature) and a circle (s→∞, constant curvature). Every stage of deliberate acceleration is a deformation of the path before it.

Conformal Map ↔ Morphism

f: C → C, f'(z) ≠ 0

A conformal map is a morphism in the category of Riemann surfaces — it preserves the structure that matters (angles) while transforming everything else. Structure-preserving change.

Droste Effect ↔ Fixed Point

z ↦ q · z (mod lattice)

The Droste spiral's self-similarity is a fixed point of the scaling map. Escher's gallery loops back into itself — like Banach's theorem, the iteration converges to exactly one image.

exp(z) ↔ Cobordism

ez: strips → annuli

The complex exponential maps horizontal strips to annuli — it's a cobordism between the flat additive world and the curved multiplicative one. Two geometries, one smooth surface connecting them.

log(z) ↔ Boundary

log: C* → C/2πi

The complex logarithm unrolls circles into lines, revealing periodicity. It exposes the boundary: where the smooth world breaks down (at z=0) is where meaning concentrates. ∂∂ = 0.

Composition ↔ Braid

f ∘ g ∘ h ≠ f ∘ h ∘ g

Composing conformal maps doesn't always commute — the order matters, like braid crossings. Each composition is a new strand in the weave, and the final pattern depends on the sequence.
